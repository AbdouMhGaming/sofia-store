---
name: Retail reporting basis
description: Why merchandise activity, actual payments and courier balances use distinct recognition dates.
---

Keep three separate concepts: merchandise activity at physical handover/return,
actual shop money when a receipt/refund is recorded, and courier debt as of a cutoff.
An online physical return reverses merchandise revenue once; its later financial
refund changes money, not merchandise revenue again.

**Why:** Courier delivery can precede payment; a shop may refund the customer before
the courier pays the shop. Treating the subsequent payout as another sale, or the
refund as debt cancellation, misstates retail results.

**How to apply:** New reports and accounting integrations must state their date
basis explicitly. Do not silently reinterpret an operational margin as cash profit
or tax/accounting profit. Fee settlements can fall in another period from delivery.

Never reverse a cost that was not expensed. An undelivered shipment returned
sellable has no sale/COGS reversal; damaged units are a loss at physical return.
Only real sellable restocks reverse saved sold costs.

**Why:** Applying a normal sales-return reversal to an unfulfilled shipment creates
fictitious profit. Damaged returns do not restore inventory value.

**How to apply:** Preserve original saved costs and distinguish pre-delivery loss
from return of a completed sale. Never infer restock decisions from free-text notes.
